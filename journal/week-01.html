Where New York Is Greener and Less Dense

The question

In New York City, lower population density and greater green land cover tend to occur together because of differences in urban development and land use.

The data

Which layers you used in the Site Scanner, what years they are from, and who made them. Then the part people skip: what is missing from them, and what each layer is standing in for. I used two layers from the Site Scanner: People (population density) and Land cover.The population density layer is from WorldPop (2020). The land cover layer is from ESA WorldCover, 10 m, 2021.

The method

I selected New York in the Site Scanner and examined the city at a citywide scale using Sentinel-2 satellite imagery as the underlying reference layer. I first displayed the People (population density) layer at an opacity of approximately 0.70, and then examined the Land cover layer at an opacity of approximately 0.45.

What I found

The two maps suggest a spatial relationship between population density and the distribution of green land cover in New York City. Lower-density areas tend to contain larger and more continuous areas of vegetation. This pattern is especially visible in parts of eastern Queens and the area around Jamaica Bay.

However, the pattern is not universal. Central Park is a clear exception: it contains extensive green land cover within Manhattan and is in close proximity to some of the city's most densely populated neighborhoods.

What this does not show

These datasets also simplify the real landscape into pixels or grid cells. The population layer measures people within approximately 100-meter cells, while the land-cover layer uses 10-meter pixels. As a result, the maps do not show what is happening within each individual cell. A single population cell may contain buildings, roads, and small green spaces at the same time, so local differences can be hidden by the spatial resolution of the data.
