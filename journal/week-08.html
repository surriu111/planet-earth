Digital Infrastructure and the City Network

The question

What digital infrastructure is mapped near my Sydney site, around the Royal Botanic Garden, and what can these records tell us about the networks supporting the city? I examine this question at two spatial scales: the immediate CBD area and a wider part of metropolitan Sydney.

The data

I used three sources, accessed on November 4, 2026. The first was the TeleGeography Submarine Cable Map, published by TeleGeography. Its map screenshots are available under a CC BY-SA 4.0 licence, and screenshots may be reproduced with attribution. The underlying database is restricted, and the interactive map is not embeddable.

The second source was OpenStreetMap data retrieved through Overpass Turbo, contributed by OSM volunteers. The data are available under the Open Database License (ODbL) 1.0, and rendered maps may be published with attribution and a licence notice. Shared derivative databases are also subject to ODbL terms.

The third source was the Data Center Map, published by Data Center Map ApS. Its content is subject to restrictive terms of use, and it cannot be reproduced without prior written permission.

The method

I ran three Overpass queries. The first mapped electricity infrastructure in the study area: 

[out:json][timeout:60];
(
  nwr["power"="substation"]({{bbox}});
  nwr["power"="tower"]({{bbox}});
);
out center;

The second mapped telecom and data-centre infrastructure across the wider Sydney extent: 

[out:json][timeout:120];
(
  nwr["telecom"]({{bbox}});
  nwr["building"="data_center"]({{bbox}});
);
out center;

The third was a narrower telecom-only query for anything at all carrying a telecom key, whatever its value, not limited to landing stations, data centres, or other specific categories: 

[out:json][timeout:60];
nwr["telecom"]({{bbox}});
out center;

These queries were used to compare the immediate CBD context with a wider urban network and to identify how public mapping captures digital infrastructure around the Royal Botanic Garden site.

Server: https://overpass-api.de/api/ (Overpass API). Retrieved November 4, 2026.

The evidence

What I found

The telecom query returned 58 records across the wider Sydney area, with telephone exchanges appearing frequently, alongside a smaller number of data centres and a cable landing station. This uneven representation suggests that different types of digital infrastructure are not equally documented in OpenStreetMap. Repeated facility names also show that the number of records does not necessarily equal the number of distinct facilities.

The power infrastructure maps reveal another contrast. Compared with the dense, relatively regular corridors of mapped transmission towers in the Shanghai tutorial, Sydney's mapped infrastructure appears more dispersed and less geometrically regular. This may partly reflect differences in urban geography, but also differences in mapping coverage and the extent of each query.

What remains absent is the network itself. Although OSM identifies individual facilities, it does not establish their actual connections, underground transmission routes, or available capacity. The mapped points therefore reveal where some infrastructure has been recorded, rather than how Sydney's complete digital and electricity networks function.

What this does not show

I am using mapped electricity infrastructure to stand for the grid accessible to my Sydney site, and it might fail because volunteer mapping based partly on visible features can represent overhead infrastructure while omitting underground connections and information about actual grid capacity.
