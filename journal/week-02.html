Climate in Sydney compared to other Australian cities

The question

How does Sydney's seasonal temperature and rainfall pattern compare with broader regional patterns across Australia?

The data

I used the World Historical Climate - Monthly Averages for GHCN-D Stations for 1981-2010 layer published by Esri in ArcGIS Online. The layer is based on NOAA/NCEI's Global Historical Climatology Network-Daily (GHCN-Daily) data and contains monthly mean temperature and precipitation for weather stations around the world. I accessed the layer on September 16, 2026. Its climate normal covers 1981-2010, which is older than the current WMO normal of 1991-2020.

One limitation is that the documentation does not fully match the live data. The layer description reports 4,620 stations, while the live attribute table contains 7,480 records. This suggests that the dataset has been updated but parts of the description still refer to an earlier version.

The method

I first filtered the climate layer to Australia using the expression GHCN-D ID starts with AS. I check some stations to compare broad regional patterns, especially coastal versus inland and northern versus southern Australia. I then added a second filter for the Sydney weather station using its GHCN-D station ID ASN00066037, joining the two expressions with AND so that only one station that is closest to Sydney remained. It is near to the Airport with name of SYDNEY AIRPORT AMO.

For the Sydney station, I created two bar charts using the twelve monthly temperature fields and the twelve monthly precipitation fields, with aggregation set to Mean. I then compared Sydney's seasonal temperature and rainfall pattern with the broader regional pattern visible in the Australia-filtered map.

The evidence

What I found

Sydney shows a moderate seasonal climate pattern, with warm summers, cool winters, and rainfall throughout the year. Compared with other Australian stations I examined, Sydney is less extreme than inland locations. Inland climate tend to have hotter summers, colder winters, and much lower precipitation.

Stations on the western coast generally showed cooler summer temperatures than Sydney, while winter temperatures and rainfall were relatively similar. In northern Australia, temperatures varied less through the year because of the lower latitude. Farther south, and especially at higher-elevation stations, winter temperatures were lower and in some cases fell below 0°C. At the Sydney station, the lowest daily temperature remained slightly above freezing, at about 0.8°C.

Overall, Sydney fits a relatively mild and moist coastal pattern: it is less seasonally extreme than inland Australia, more seasonal than the tropical north, and warmer in winter than many southern or high-elevation locations.

What this does not show

I originally intended to aggregate the other Australian regional stations into a single monthly average for comparison with Sydney. However, the chart category made it difficult to produce combined seasonal temperature and precipitation profile for regional stations. As a result, my regional comparison is based on examining several individual stations rather than a regional average.
